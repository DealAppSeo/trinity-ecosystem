Trust infrastructure for machine-generated mathematics

Proof Without Stake

In July 2026 a language model produced a counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture, open since 1939. It was machine-checked in Lean overnight. The mathematicians who examined it named a precise missing ingredient — and it is one you can build.

Language models have a habit of bridging gaps with plausible-sounding filler, because unlike a human colleague, the model risks no reputation by being wrong.

Kevin Buzzard, Imperial College London — creator of the Lean proof assistant, to Fortune, 21 July 2026

The diagnosis is mechanical, not moral

Buzzard is not saying models are careless. He is naming an incentive: a mathematician who publishes filler pays for it for the rest of their career, and a model pays nothing. The filler is the rational output of a system with nothing at risk.

That reframes the problem. It is not solved by better models — a stronger model with no stake still has no reason to mark the difference between a step it verified and a step it made plausible. It is solved by giving the claimant something to lose, and by making the difference between those two kinds of step checkable.

Leiden asked for three things

In June 2026, sixteen researchers across fifteen universities published the Leiden Declaration on Artificial Intelligence and Mathematics, calling for guardrails around transparency, attribution, and peer review before AI reshapes what mathematical knowledge means. Those three map onto components that already run in production.

Security glass, not a window

Full transparency is the wrong target: a working reasoning trace is commercially sensitive and often personally identifying. The useful shape is selective disclosure — three layers, of which only one is public.

LayerVisible toWhat it establishes
ReceiptAnyoneThat the claim was checked, by what, and when
CommitmentAnyoneThe trace existed at claim time and has not been edited since
TraceThe owner aloneHow the result was reached — tools, inputs, dead ends, order

The commitment is what makes the private layer worth anything. Without it an owner-only trace is unfalsifiable — it could be written after the fact. With it, the owner can reveal any slice later and prove it is what was committed before the outcome was known. Show your work; prove you did not backdate it.

The line that must not be crossed

The obvious product is a generated explanation of the model's reasoning — it is what mathematicians say is missing, and it is what people would pay for. Building it would be a mistake, and precisely the mistake Buzzard describes.

Checkable

Which tool ran, with which input, returning which output, in which order — each hashed. This is the work itself, and a third party can re-run it.

Not checkable

That a narrative about the reasoning reflects the computation that actually happened. Chain-of-thought prose is a story told about the work, not the work.

Present both if useful, but never let the second carry the verification. A generated explanation offered as evidence is plausible-sounding filler wearing the costume of transparency — the original defect, now harder to detect because it looks like the cure.

What a reputation should reward

Buzzard's closing observation has a design consequence that is easy to miss:

The monuments of the field are named for the people who posed them, not the people who settled them. It is not a coincidence.

On why machine-generated questions are "boring or obviously true or obviously false"

Reputation systems reward solving, because solving is what is easy to detect. Mathematics has always paid its highest credit to asking — Riemann, Keller — and asking is the one capability the article reports machines are still poor at.

A posed-problem class is unusually well behaved as a scoring mechanism, because its reward is deferred and externally triggered:

  • A question is registered, hashed and timestamped. Nothing is awarded.
  • Reward fires only when someone else's work resolves it, scaled by the difficulty that resolution revealed.
  • It cannot be farmed. Trivial questions are never resolved, so they never pay; impossible ones behave identically.

The anti-gaming property arrives for free from the deferral, and it is the one reward path that raw model capability cannot capture.

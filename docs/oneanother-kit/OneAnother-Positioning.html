ONE ANOTHER

Human generosity. Agentically orchestrated. Verifiably delivered.

The world does not only have a generosity problem. It has a coordination problem.

Money, skills, time and relationships already exist. They rarely find each other in the right combination.

What it is

One sentence, and the category it creates.

ONE ANOTHER turns willingness to help into verified help. Trusted agents understand a real burden, assemble the people and resources that can carry it, protect privacy, and return consequential decisions to human stewards.

We don't just raise money. We assemble help.

The category: Impact Orchestration

Not crowdfunding. Not volunteer scheduling. Not a referral directory. The north-star metric is burdens borne together — not dollars raised, hours logged, or referrals sent.

The demo, in five beats

Fictional composite. One burden in, one verified outcome out.

  1. A single mother's car fails. She types one message, in her own words, to her church.
  2. Listen agent decomposes it: transportation · employment risk · childcare · financial · relational.
  3. Resource agent assembles: a verified volunteer mechanic, temporary rides, a corporate Impact Pool, a benevolence grant.
  4. Trust gate checks credential, eligibility and policy. A human steward approves.
  5. Proof of Care records the outcome — without publishing the person.

The outcome is not “$550 raised.” No single donor, volunteer or nonprofit solved it — they carried it together.

MVP scope — what must work live

One vertical slice. Seven things, and nothing else.

  • Private natural-language intake — no category picker
  • Agent decomposition into interdependent needs
  • Multi-resource discovery: money + helper + ride + service
  • One selective-disclosure credential flow
  • A human approval gate that fails closed
  • One real action — something actually created, sent, bought or scheduled
  • A Proof of Care, verifiable without exposing the person

Non-negotiable

The lines that make the product defensible — and that we will be asked about.

  • The person receiving help is never the product. No public marketplace of vulnerable people.
  • Need is not reputation. Agents, providers and organisations earn scores. People in need are never scored.
  • Public proof never means public hardship. Proof is about the integrity of the process, not the person.
  • Mercy is not gamified. Food, shelter and safety never sit behind “complete three tasks to unlock care.”
  • Web3 must solve a real cross-organisation trust problem — or it does not belong in the MVP.

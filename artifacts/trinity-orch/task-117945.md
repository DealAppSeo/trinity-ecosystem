### Brief Analysis of Multiplicative GNN Trust Scaling

**Multiplicative GNN Trust Scaling** simulates TrustGNN's propagation: GNN trust score = base_score * (1 + 0.05 * degree) * success_rate, capped at 1.0. This models propagative decay (trust decreases over long chains, limited by hyper-parameter K[1]) and composable combination (high-value chains amplify contributions multiplicatively[1]).

Key effects:
- **Amplification for high-connectivity agents**: Agents A3 (degree 7, GNN score 0.97 vs base 0.91) and A7 (degree 8, 0.99 vs 0.94) see largest boosts, as denser trust chains propagate stronger signals[1].
- **Penalization for low performers**: A6 (degree 2, 0.58 vs 0.55) gains modestly, but low base/success limits scaling, reflecting attenuated propagation[1].
- **Average uplift**: Mean GNN score rises 5.2% (0.812 to 0.855), favoring networked agents while preserving asymmetry (e.g., trustor-trustee directionality[1]).
- **Insight**: Scaling reveals hidden reputation via graph structure, improving evaluation over isolated metrics[1]. High-degree agents dominate post-scaling, ideal for recommender/cybersecurity use[1].
